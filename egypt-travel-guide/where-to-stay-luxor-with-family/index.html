Where to Stay in Luxor With Kids: East Bank, West Bank or a Private Apartment?

With children, the winning location is the one that reduces heat, waiting and room friction—not necessarily the one closest to the city centre.

Published 2026-08-07 · Updated 2026-08-07

What families actually need in Luxor

Ancient sites are only part of the decision. Families need somewhere children can rest between early starts, somewhere adults can sit after bedtime, and transport that does not depend on repeated negotiation. Separate bedrooms, a pool, simple food and reliable air conditioning often matter more than a grand lobby. Build the accommodation choice around the hottest part of the day: if everyone can return for lunch and a swim, the family can enjoy another short outing later instead of exhausting itself in one continuous tour.

East Bank with children

The East Bank offers the largest hotels, more restaurants and easier access to Luxor Temple, Karnak and the corniche. It can suit first-time families who want a conventional resort or expect to walk out for dinner. The disadvantages are city traffic, busier surroundings and a longer start for West Bank tombs or balloons. Large hotels may have excellent pools and children’s facilities, but family room layouts should be checked carefully; “family room” can still mean everyone sleeping in one space.

West Bank with children

The West Bank is quieter and puts the Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut’s temple and balloon launch areas on the same side of the Nile. Village roads and fields feel more relaxed after Cairo or a cruise. The trade-off is that East Bank evenings require a ferry or road journey, and some rural properties are isolated without host transport. Choose a property that explains ferry access, meals and pickups clearly. Royal Nile Villas offers a shuttle to the ferry and can arrange private vehicles for sightseeing.

Why an apartment can work better

A two-bedroom apartment lets children sleep while adults use the living room, and a kitchen handles early breakfasts, familiar snacks or dietary needs. It can also reduce the cost and uncertainty of booking two hotel rooms. Royal Nile Villas units sleep up to four, with different combinations of queen and single beds. Some have one bathroom and some have two. Families should choose based on the real bed plan and children’s ages, not only the apartment photographs.

Pool, shade and summer planning

A pool is most useful as part of the daily schedule, not as a decorative amenity. Tour tombs or temples at opening, return before the strongest heat, eat and rest, then use the pool when conditions are appropriate. Royal Nile Villas has a 14-metre shared pool and garden. Children require active adult supervision; do not assume lifeguard coverage at a small accommodation property. In summer, schedule shorter outings and carry more water than the children normally drink at home.

A family-friendly three-day rhythm

On day one, settle in and take only a gentle sunset Nile activity. On day two, start early on the West Bank and return for lunch and pool time. On day three, visit Karnak early, rest, then see Luxor Temple after the heat softens. Add the balloon only if every child meets the operator’s age, height and safety requirements and is comfortable with the pre-dawn start. Never hide a child’s age to secure a booking.

Questions to ask before booking

Ask about cot availability, stair access, balcony security, pool rules, the exact kitchen, blackout curtains, vehicle child restraints and medical access. Egypt’s vehicle-seat practices may differ from home, so request what you need rather than assuming it. Confirm whether breakfast is included or available, how late dinner runs and who helps if a child becomes ill. Clear answers are more valuable than a generic “family friendly” label.

Booking checklist
  • Choose separate bedrooms when bedtime matters.
  • Ask for the exact bed plan and bathroom count.
  • Plan one major outing per morning.
  • Confirm pool supervision and balcony arrangements.
  • Request appropriate vehicle restraints in advance.
  • Keep familiar snacks and rehydration supplies in the apartment.
