Where to Stay in Luxor for a Week: Why a Short-Term Apartment Makes Sense

A week transforms Luxor from a checklist into a place to live slowly—if your accommodation supports ordinary daily life.

Published 2026-08-07 · Updated 2026-08-07

A week is not too long

Luxor has enough archaeology for several days, but the real benefit of a week is pacing. You can visit exposed sites at opening, rest through the strongest heat, take slower meals and leave room for weather or simple curiosity. A conventional hotel room can begin to feel restrictive by day four. An apartment gives you living space and a kitchen, making the quiet hours part of the stay rather than dead time between tours.

Choose a neighbourhood, not just a property

For a week, daily surroundings matter. The East Bank offers urban convenience and a larger restaurant choice. The West Bank offers rural calm, tomb access and a more residential rhythm. Ask how you will reach groceries, the ferry and evening meals. Royal Nile Villas combines an Al Aqaletah village location with a ferry shuttle and on-site restaurant, so guests can stay quietly without being completely dependent on taxis or cooking.

The apartment checklist for seven nights

Confirm a genuine kitchen, refrigerator, comfortable seating, reliable Wi-Fi, air conditioning in the bedrooms, housekeeping frequency, towels, laundry options and enough storage to unpack. Photographs should show the actual unit, not only shared gardens. Royal Nile Villas has several two-bedroom apartments and penthouses, but floor, view, beds and bathrooms differ. Select the unit that fits how you will live for the week.

Working remotely from Luxor

If part of the stay is a working week, ask about the workspace and test assumptions about video calls. Wi-Fi reliability can vary anywhere, so keep mobile data as a backup. A top-floor view is inspiring, but stairs, afternoon heat and desk ergonomics may matter more. Sky Penthouse 2 includes a dedicated workspace; confirm current internet arrangements and any critical requirements before booking rather than discovering them during a meeting.

A slow seven-day plan

Use day one for arrival, day two for the West Bank, day three for rest and an evening temple, day four for Karnak and the museum, day five for Dendera or another regional excursion, day six for a balloon or Nile sail, and day seven for a flexible favourite or onward travel. This arrangement intentionally leaves open afternoons. Luxor rewards returning to a place in different light more than racing through an extra monument at midday.

Food without cooking every day

A kitchen is useful even if you never prepare dinner. It handles fruit, cold drinks, breakfast before an early tour and meals for travellers with dietary needs. Combine groceries with local restaurants and one or two meals at the Royal Nile View Restaurant. Ask about allergy procedures directly; “vegetarian available” does not automatically answer a severe-allergy question.

Rates, cleaning and expectations

Longer stays may have different rates or cleaning arrangements, but never assume a weekly discount. Ask what is included, how often the apartment is serviced, whether utilities or extra guests change the rate and which cancellation terms apply. If you are booking more than one apartment, request a precise rooming plan. A transparent weekly quote is more useful than a low nightly figure with unclear extras.
